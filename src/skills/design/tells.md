# Tells: defaults that read as generated

Models trained on the same templates converge on the same handful of looks. Each of these is legitimate when a brief calls for it; each is a failure when it appears by habit, regardless of subject. Where the brief leaves a choice free, don't spend it on one of these. When you catch one in your own work, ask what this specific product would do instead.

## Palettes
- Warm cream background (around #F4F1EA) with a high-contrast serif display and a terracotta or clay accent (around #D97757). It has become a default of generated pages, so on a client's brief it reads as a tell.
- Near-black background (#0B0B0B, #111) with one bright acid-green, electric-lime or vermilion accent.
- Purple-to-blue gradients, "AI purple", neon glows, and gradient washes as decoration.
- Pure grey neutrals with no temperature, and grey text on a coloured surface (tint secondary text toward the surface's hue instead).
- Instead: derive the palette from the subject's materials and world, give neutrals a temperature, keep one accent with a job, and keep saturation in check.

## Type
- Inter, Roboto, Arial or the system stack for everything, especially with slate-900 text.
- One word in a headline set apart in italic, bold or a different colour.
- All-caps, letter-spaced labels; an eyebrow (kicker) above every heading.
- A monospace face for small data labels as a "technical" costume, when nothing is code or tabular.
- Gradient text.
- Instead: faces chosen for this subject, a real scale, the headline's own size and weight doing the work, sentence case labels only where a label helps.

## Layout
- The centred hero over a dark mesh with a big number, a small label, supporting stats and a gradient accent.
- Three equal feature cards with an icon tile, a heading and two lines of text; then the same again.
- Broadsheet styling (hairline rules, zero radius, dense newspaper columns) on a product that isn't editorial.
- Numbered markers (01 / 02 / 03) on content that isn't a sequence.
- Instead: open with the most characteristic thing in the subject's world, in whatever form fits it (a headline, an image, a live demo, an interaction); let the structure follow the content's real shape.

## The SaaS card kit
- Everything chopped into identical rounded cards, one radius on everything regardless of hierarchy, the same soft grey shadow (rgba(0,0,0,.1)) under each.
- Cards nested inside cards (always wrong), coloured borders thicker than 1px, a rounded-square icon tile above every heading.
- Glass and blur as decoration; colour halos without offset passed off as depth.
- Instead: group with space and alignment first, use a container only when it means "one thing", vary radius and elevation by role, and make shadows read as light (offset plus blur, two layers).

## Template chrome
- Meta strings joined with middle dots ("A · B · C") everywhere.
- Labels built as "WORD — fragment" with a spaced em dash.
- An arrow appended to every link and button ("Learn more →").
- Sparklines, progress rings and soft rectangles standing in for real content.
- Emoji or Unicode glyphs instead of an icon set; mixed icon styles.
- Instead: real content, one icon set used consistently, and structure that encodes information (borders, numbers, dividers and labels only where they say something).

## Motion
- Fade-and-slide-up entrances on every section, hover lifts on every card, infinite micro-animations, bounce and elastic easing.
- Instead: one orchestrated moment (a page-load sequence or a reveal), and motion that answers what the person did (motion skill).

## Copy
- "Welcome to the future of…", "Unlock", "Seamless", "Supercharge", "Elevate", "Effortlessly".
- Labels named after the system ("Webhook config") instead of what the person does ("Notifications").
- Instead: say plainly what it is and what happens, in the product's own vocabulary (ux-writing skill).

## Using this list
- During planning: if a part of your plan matches an item here and the brief didn't ask for it, revise it and say why.
- During critique: scan the screenshot for each item. One tell is a note; three is a redesign of that part.
- Don't overcorrect into a new default: avoiding cream doesn't mean everything becomes sage. The goal is choices made for this brief.
